Detailed Description

The CT filter: a low-pass of the current added back with a gain, one per phase.

A current transformer acts as a high-pass filter. Robin's sketches offset it with an IIR low-pass filter of the current, added back with a gain (lpf_gain, alpha in calibration.h). This is his filter, Mk2_fasterControl_Full/processing.cpp, in fixed point instead of his float maths (two float multiplies and four conversions took ~1100 cycles of the ADC interrupt, per phase; this takes ~210):

  • his state, at x256 scale (one ADC count = 256): it moves as soon as alpha x the difference reaches one unit (3.6 ADC counts with alpha 0.0011);
  • his update, lpf += alpha x (sample - lpf), truncated towards zero as his float to int32 conversion does. That truncation holds his state up to 0.5 / alpha units short of the input's mean (1.8 counts with alpha 0.0011, 16 counts at the output with a gain of 9): the filter the old boards were tuned with, kept as it is;
  • alpha and the gain turned into fixed point at compile time (coefficientsOf()), from the float settings of calibration.h: alpha x 2^(16 + shift), gain x 2^8.

The firmware's sample is DC-removed and left-aligned (x64 scale, int16): it is filtered at x256, the output is back at x64, saturated to the int16 range, so that a peak beyond full scale cannot wrap and flip the sign of the power.

Pure C++, no Arduino: the native tests include it.
